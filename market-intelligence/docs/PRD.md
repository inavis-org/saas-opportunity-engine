# Vision

Build the market intelligence platform every SaaS founder wishes existed.

Instead of spending hours researching competitors, reading reviews, browsing Reddit, comparing pricing pages, and guessing what customers actually want, founders should receive actionable market intelligence within minutes.

The platform should answer one simple question:

> **"What should I build next, and why?"**

Unlike traditional competitive intelligence tools that focus on monitoring competitors, this platform focuses on discovering validated business opportunities backed by real customer feedback.

---

# Problem Statement

## Background

Every successful SaaS begins with identifying a real customer problem.

Today, validating a market is a slow, fragmented, and highly manual process.

Founders typically perform research by visiting multiple websites including review platforms, online communities, social media, product directories, documentation, and pricing pages.

They manually collect information into spreadsheets before asking AI tools to summarize it.

This process is repetitive, time-consuming, inconsistent, and difficult to repeat for multiple markets.

Most importantly, it rarely produces a confident answer to the question:

> **"Is this a worthwhile opportunity to build?"**

---

## Current Workflow

A founder wants to build software in a specific category.

Their research process usually looks like this:

* Search competitors
* Read hundreds of customer reviews
* Browse Reddit discussions
* Read feature requests
* Compare pricing
* Watch YouTube reviews
* Read Product Hunt comments
* Copy everything into documents
* Ask ChatGPT for summaries
* Repeat for every competitor

This research can take anywhere from several hours to multiple days.

The output depends heavily on the individual's research ability and often misses recurring patterns hidden across thousands of reviews.

---

## Core Problems

Founders currently struggle because:

### Information is fragmented

Customer feedback exists across dozens of websites.

There is no unified view.

---

### Research is slow

Reading hundreds of reviews manually is expensive in terms of time.

Many founders stop researching before they have enough evidence.

---

### Patterns are difficult to identify

Humans are poor at recognizing recurring themes across thousands of reviews.

Important trends are easily missed.

---

### Market validation is subjective

Most founders rely on intuition rather than evidence.

They often build products based on assumptions instead of validated customer pain.

---

### Existing tools solve different problems

Current competitive intelligence platforms primarily target:

* Product Managers
* Sales Teams
* Marketing Teams
* Enterprise organizations

Very few products help solo founders answer:

> **"What should I build?"**

---

# Opportunity

Every customer complaint represents friction.

Every repeated complaint represents demand.

Every repeated feature request represents a potential product opportunity.

The market already contains millions of customer reviews describing:

* frustrations
* missing features
* pricing concerns
* usability issues
* unmet needs

The challenge is not collecting opinions.

The challenge is transforming unstructured customer feedback into structured business opportunities.

---

# Product Goal

Create a platform that converts scattered customer feedback into actionable market intelligence.

Instead of delivering summaries, the product should deliver decisions.

Users should understand:

* what customers dislike
* what customers repeatedly request
* which market gaps exist
* how competitors compare
* where demand is strongest
* which opportunities are worth pursuing

# MVP Scope

The MVP focuses on solving one workflow exceptionally well.

## Input

The user provides:

* a supported competitor review page, or
* imported review data from a supported source.

---

## Processing

The system:

* collects or imports reviews
* normalizes review data
* performs AI analysis
* extracts recurring complaints
* extracts feature requests
* groups similar feedback
* calculates basic metrics
* generates opportunity insights

---

## Output

The user receives:

Executive Summary

Complaint Analysis

Feature Requests

Sentiment Summary

Opportunity Score

Suggested Product Opportunities

Supporting Evidence

Exportable Report

---

# Out of Scope

The MVP will NOT include:

* mobile applications
* browser extensions
* enterprise permissions
* public API
* advanced team collaboration
* dozens of data sources
* automated competitor monitoring
* forecasting models
* investor dashboards

These may be explored after product-market fit.

---

# Success Metrics

The MVP is successful if:

* users complete analyses without assistance
* users understand results in under five minutes
* users return to analyze additional competitors
* users are willing to pay for repeated analyses
* users recommend the platform to other founders

Business success metrics after launch:

* First paying customer
* 10 paying customers
* 100 analyses completed
* Weekly active users
* Customer retention
* Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)

---

# Guiding Principles

Every feature must answer at least one of these questions:

* What problems exist?
* How common are they?
* Who experiences them?
* What do customers repeatedly request?
* What opportunities exist?
* Why should the user trust this conclusion?

If a feature does not help users make better product decisions, it should not be part of the MVP.

---

# Long-Term Vision

The long-term goal is not to become another review analytics tool.

The goal is to become the operating system for market research.

Eventually, the platform should continuously monitor markets, identify emerging customer pain points, track competitors, surface new opportunities, and help founders decide what to build next using evidence instead of intuition.

When founders ask:

> **"What SaaS should I build next?"**

This platform should be the first place they go.
